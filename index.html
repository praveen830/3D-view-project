What is the Milky Way?

The Milky Way is our cosmic home—a vast barred spiral galaxy containing 100 to 400 billion stars and over 100 billion planets. Spanning approximately 100,000 light-years in diameter, its luminous spiral arms are woven with nebulae, star clusters, and deep cosmic dust lanes.

At the galactic center lies Sagittarius A*, a supermassive black hole with a mass 4.15 million times our Sun. Our Solar System is situated in the Orion Spur ~26,000 light-years from the core, orbiting at 828,000 km/h and taking 230 million years to complete one Galactic Year.

What is the Solar System?

Formed 4.6 billion years ago from the gravitational collapse of a giant interstellar cloud, our Solar System comprises our host star—the Sun—and every celestial body bound by its gravity. The Sun contains 99.86% of the system's total mass, fueling planetary climates and orbital mechanics.

The system is divided into four inner rocky terrestrial planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars), the Asteroid Belt, two gas giants (Jupiter, Saturn), and two ice giants (Uranus, Neptune). All major planets orbit counterclockwise in the flat ecliptic plane, surrounded by hundreds of moons, rings, and comets.
